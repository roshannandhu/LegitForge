Ask five studios in Kerala or Bangalore what a website costs and you will get five numbers that are miles apart. Most of that gap is not greed or skill. It is that each quote is for a different thing, and most quotes do not say what that thing is.

So instead of a price list, here is what actually sets the price, and how to read a quote.

## The one question that sets the range

Does the site need to *remember* anything? A set of finished pages (your services, photos, prices, a map, a WhatsApp button) is a static site. A site that has logins, bookings against real slots, stock, quotes or warranties is a web app, because it needs a database and code on the server.

That single answer moves a quote more than anything else on this page. If you are not sure which you need, our post on [static or dynamic websites](/blog/static-or-dynamic-website) has a two-minute test.

## What you are paying for in the build

Every honest quote is made of the same parts. The bigger each one is, the higher the price.

- **Pages.** Five well-written pages and fifty thin ones are different jobs. Count the pages you really need.
- **Words and photos.** Do you have them, or does someone have to write the text and take the pictures? Copy is often the slowest part of a project.
- **Design.** A clean layout built for your business, or a ready-made theme with your logo on it. Both are fine; they are not the same price.
- **The moving parts.** Each form, booking, login, payment or admin screen is real work, and it is where most of the cost of a web app goes.
- **Connections.** Linking the site to WhatsApp, Google Sheets, a payment gateway or your billing software adds testing time.
- **Speed and SEO setup.** Titles, descriptions, a sitemap, Google Search Console and a site that loads fast on a mid-range phone. Some quotes include it; some leave it out and charge later.

## The costs that are not the developer's

These are paid to other companies, whoever builds your site. Ask that they are listed separately.

| Item | Who you pay | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Domain name (.in or .com) | A domain registrar | Every year |
| Hosting | A hosting company; a static site often costs nothing to host | Monthly or yearly |
| Business email | Google Workspace, Zoho or similar | Per person, monthly |
| WhatsApp messages | Meta, through the WhatsApp Business Platform | Per message sent |
| Payment gateway | Razorpay, Stripe or similar | A share of each payment |
| GST | Added to the bill for the work, where it applies | On the invoice |

Make sure the domain, hosting and email accounts are in **your** name. If a studio registers them under its own account, leaving later becomes slow and expensive.

## The costs after launch

A website is not finished on launch day. Before you sign, find out:

- How long fixes are free after launch, and whether that is in writing.
- What a change costs later: a new page, a new photo, a new price.
- Whether you can edit text and images yourself.
- Who renews the domain, and what happens if the renewal is missed.

A cheap build with expensive changes often costs more over two years than a fair build you can update yourself.

## Is it cheaper in Kerala than in Bangalore?

Often a little, because studios in Calicut or Kochi have lower costs than studios in Bangalore. But the price of a project depends far more on what is in it than on where the developer sits. Remote work is normal now; we are in Calicut and work with businesses in Bangalore over calls and preview links every week.

Compare quotes for the same scope, not quotes from the same city.

## How to compare two quotes

Put them side by side and check each one says:

1. Which pages and features are included, by name.
2. Who writes the text and supplies the photos.
3. Which outside costs (the table above) are included and which are yours.
4. How long the build takes, and when you see a first design.
5. How many free fixes there are after launch, and for how long.
6. That the code, domain and every account will be in your name.

If a quote cannot answer these, the number on it does not mean much yet.
